Sandra Jõgeva (1976) on interdistsiplinaarne kunstnik (BA EKA maaliosakonnast 2000, MA EKA interdistsiplinaarsete kunstide osakonnast), kes viimastel aastatel on keskendunud kunstiteemalistele dokumentaalfilmidele.

Portreedokk Mare Trallast tuli välja 2022, “Kunst ja külm sõda” 2024.

Praegu on tootmisfaasis “Ando Keskküla vaim” ja “Live Fast, Die Old” kunstnik Raul Meelest ning tema abikaasast, kirjanik Mari Saadist.

Sandra Jõgeval on 2026 algusest oma kunstiteemaline kanal Youtube’is saatega “Välja sõit/ Fieldnotes”, mis keskendub ettevõtmistele väljaspool peavoolu nii geograafilises kui muus mõttes.

Sandra Jõgeva kunstialase tegevuse poolest võib viimastel aastatel mainida projekti

„Pied-à-terre“, mis oli esmakordselt eksponeeritud tema stuudios Tallinna vanalinnas 2025 oktoobris ning leidis edasiarenduse 2026 maist juulini isiknäitusel “Kas sa tead, mis Hanif Kureishiga juhtus?” MIGAs Tartus. Ka isiknäitust “Rahva teener” galeriis Metropol Tallinnas 2026 veebruaris.	Enne seda aga osavõttu Marian Grau (enne Kivila) kuraatorinäitustest "Naine & naine" Pärnu Linnagaleriis 2019. ja 2021.a. suvel, isiknäitust " Kakskümmend aastat hiljem" Fahle galeriis 2020. lõpus samaaegselt koos installatsiooni "Seitse surmapattu" eksponeerimisega Solarise galeriis. Samuti osavõttu isiknäitusega "Seitse surmapattu" Pärnu Fotofestil 2020, osavõttu XIII Balti Triennaalist Riias, 2018.a.

2021. a. oktoobris pandi Youtube'i keskkonda üles selle jaoks valminud videoteos "Minu seiklused Eesti sotsiaalsüsteemis (koos Teet Raudsepaga), juunis tuli välja Sandra Jõgeva kolmas raamat, lühiromaan "Üks päev aastal 0" (Rahva Raamat).

Sandra Jõgeva on pälvinud Eesti Kultuurkapitali audiovisuaalse kunsti sihtkapitali preemia oma debüütfilmi "Armastus..." eest (2018), sama filmi eest ka EFTA aasta dokumentaalfilmi auhinna ning Dokumentalistide Gildi kolleegipreemia Doki Kild; Kulka kujutava ja sihtkapitali preemia 2004 rühmituse Avangard tegevuse eest koos Margus Tammega ning Betti Alveri debüüdipreemia novellikogu "Draamapunkt" eest 2009.



Sandra Jõgeva (b. 1976) is an interdisciplinary artist. She holds a BA from the Painting Department of the Estonian Academy of Arts (EKA), awarded in 2000, and an MA from EKA’s Department of Interdisciplinary Arts. In recent years, she has increasingly focused on documentary films dealing with art and artists.

Her documentary portrait of artist Mare Tralla was released in 2022, followed by _Art and the Cold War_ in 2024. She is currently in production with _The Ghost of Ando Keskküla_ and _Live Fast, Die Old_, a film about artist Raul Meel and his wife, writer Mari Saat.

Since early 2026, Sandra Jõgeva has also run her own YouTube channel, _Välja sõit / Fieldnotes_, an art-focused programme exploring activities and phenomena outside the mainstream, both geographically and in broader cultural terms.

In terms of her artistic practice, notable recent projects include _Pied-à-terre_, first exhibited in her studio in Tallinn’s Old Town in October 2025 and subsequently developed further for her solo exhibition _Do You Know What Happened to Hanif Kureishi?_, held at MIGA in Tartu from May to July 2026. Another recent solo exhibition was _Servant of the People_, presented at Metropol Gallery in Tallinn in February 2026.

Earlier projects include participation in Marian Grau (formerly Kivila)’s curated exhibitions _Woman & Woman_ at Pärnu City Gallery in the summers of 2019 and 2021; the solo exhibition _Twenty Years Later_ at Fahle Gallery in late 2020, presented simultaneously with the installation _The Seven Deadly Sins_ at Solaris Gallery; and participation in Pärnu Fotofest 2020 with the solo exhibition _The Seven Deadly Sins_. She also participated in the 13th Baltic Triennial in Riga in 2018.

In October 2021, the video work _My Adventures in the Estonian Social Welfare System_ (with Teet Raudsep) was released on YouTube. In June, Jõgeva published her third book, the novella _One Day in Year 0_ (Rahva Raamat).

Sandra Jõgeva has received several awards and grants for her work. She was awarded the Estonian Cultural Endowment’s Audiovisual Arts Endowment prize for her debut film _Love..._ (2018), as well as the EFTA Award for Documentary of the Year and the Documentary Guild’s peer award, Doki Kild, for the same film. In 2004, she received the Estonian Cultural Endowment’s Visual Arts Endowment prize for the activities of the artist collective Avangard, together with Margus Tamm. In 2009, she received the Betti Alver Debut Prize for her short-story collection _Draamapunkt_.
